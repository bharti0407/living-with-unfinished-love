ABOUT THE AUTHOR

She never planned to become a writer. She simply had too many feelings and nowhere left to keep them.

Through love , distance, silence, and unanswered questions, he became the reason this book was written.

Every page of living with unfinished love is a quiet reflection of her own journey - of loving deeply , losing silently, and learning that some people leave without ever truly leaving our hearts. There was someone who unknowingly became the reason these words were written, but this book is not about him. It is about her waiting days, memories and her courage to turn heartbreak into something beautiful.

This is her first book, written with honesty and the hope that her story will forever remind her how far she has come.

There was once a person who changed my life without ever realizing it.

He taught me what love felt like, but he also taught me how silence can hurt more than words. He was my safest place for a while, and then he became the hardest place to return to. I don't write these pages to blame him or to ask him back. I write because he unknowingly gave me a story that my heart refused to forget.

If these words ever find him, I hope he knows he was loved—truly, deeply, and without conditions. And even though our story remained unfinished, it became the beginning of the woman I am today.

I saw him for the first time in college. Relationships have always given me pain, that is why I always ran away from relationships. So, as always, I tried my best to escape from him, but I don't know why it did not happen this time.

Like Bollywood movies, we started talking through our eyes, connected on social media, became friends, and then expressed our love. Everything was going well, but as they say, the evil eye always ruins everything.

I experienced for the first time why a person is ready to lose everything in love.

“After promises of spending a lifetime together, we both went our separate ways”. He probably forgot, he left, but I remained there in the same place, hoping for him, waiting for him, dreaming of moving forward with him. Yes, I know I am waiting for something that will never come, and that wait is still on... because I can't forget him.

because maybe I don't want to forget him. It is not necessary that we should leave what we cannot get. Many times I think that I should forget him, but has anyone been able to forget their own self?

I used to feel peace sitting next to him. For a moment, my face used to smile on seeing him. I love even waiting for him. How can I forget him?

What is love if you forget it?

“That's why I collected every moment we spent together, those lovely memories, and kept them within myself.” There is no sign of his return; there is a wait that never ends.

It is said that if one of the two people does not give up, God will bring them together again. It is said that stories are not meant to be incomplete; they are meant to be complete.

The Silence after him

Some memories refuse to leave. They sit quietly in the corners of your heart whispering of love long gone.

After a person leaves, only a silence remains behind — and within that silence the live memories of that person. Memories that sometimes makes you cry, sometimes hurt deeply and sometimes completely break you. Because that relationship came into your life at a time when you had already made that person your everything and the goodbye happened at a moment when you least expected it. A person who was once cheerful, always smiling, living with a childlike innocence and finding happiness in small moments began to live in solitude. Not because there was no one around, but because the one they loved the most had left their life. What remained were only memories – memories that punished them every single day.

That silence breaks a person up to a certain point, but slowly, the same silence teaches them how to heal. After that one person left, you made silence your home — and that isn't easy, is it? It wasn't easy for me either. That silence slowly destroys a person from within, kills them and completely changes someone who was once happy and full of life. Expecting anything from people always brings pain, so they learn to live, walk and survive alone. They say that with time everything gets better, but that's not really true. A person simply learns how to live with the pain. That's why, in that silence, they begin to search for themselves.

Some relationships start with words and end in silence. They give you happiness in the beginning, but in the end, only silence remains — you, the things they once said, the memories, the promises and that quietness which never lets you feel truly alone, but makes you die a little every moment in their memory.

But the truth is that an ending is never truly complete; it simply takes on a new form—one such silence that does not speak, yet says so much without words. In that moment, the pain does not make noise; it becomes a person's quietness. It teaches us that not every pain needs words. Earlier, in every silent moment, their memories would return—their laughter, their anger, their habits. Everything felt as if even the memories knew there was no one left to speak to, so they knocked harder, like a stronger wind...

But slowly , a person learns that some memories cannot be chased away ; they can only be endured.

People say that with time one forgets, stops loving - but that's not true.

A person simply gets tired, and that's why they stop showing, telling and speaking....

THE ART OF WAITING

They say everyone can fall in love, but not everyone is capable of waiting. True love is that where, even after losing someone, there is still hope and patience for their return. Waiting for someone to come back who has already left your life, yet even today our waiting for them remains unchanged. The relationship has ended, but the waiting has not. A person whom you are perhaps destined to have, and yet losing or forgetting them is not acceptable to you —Still, you wait. yes, because this is love. On this journey of waiting, you break a thousand times, yet your waiting never comes to an end.

They say that waiting for someone dear, someone you love is one of those experiences in life that is very difficult to put into words, and feeling it is even deeper.

On this journey, the mind questions itself every moment, the heart swings between fear and hope, and time seems to move deliberately slowly. When we wait for someone dear to us, every day begins with the same thought—maybe today—and every night ends with an incomplete thought—maybe tomorrow. And in these maybes we begin to pass our days. Life feels as if it is merely being endured; you are not really living it. Because for the one we consider our own, we are willing to stop and pause.

While waiting for someone dear, the morning sunlight feels different, and the evenings seem to grow longer because there is no one to talk to. “Nights become even more difficult, because in that darkness, their memories begin to make even more noise.” This is what love truly is. Sometimes, living in memories brings more peace than facing the truth. The moment a phone notification rings, the heart starts beating faster.

An unknown call awakens hope, but not seeing their name brings silence once again. This waiting teaches us patience, but it is not easy, because every person has a limit that is often tested through anger, exhaustion and breaking down. Each time, the act of waiting tests that limit again and again.

Sometimes we ask ourselves whether our waiting is justified — whether the person we are waiting for remembers us too, or whether it is only us carrying the weight of emotions. But the heart does not settle on any answer, because in love, logic always loses. The most difficult moments of this waiting are when we begin to feel that perhaps our wait could be endless, that there may be no end date for it....No promises of their return. Yet, we stopped.

“This waiting is not just the hope of someone's return, but also an effort to protect ourselves from breaking apart. Sometimes the waiting never ends; we simply learn to live with it.”

Section 07

Living with Unfinished Love

They say that staying devoted to one person, and remaining theirs even after they are gone, is not something everyone can do. Everyone can fall in love, but not everyone can wait. Anyone can confess their love, but not everyone can turn it into devotion. It is easy to stay loyal while someone is still with you, but to remain only theirs even after they have left — that is difficult. Everyone tells me, "You'll find many others," but who wants to search for many? I want the one to whom I belong. People compare their loved one with someone else so easily... how do I explain to them that my love is such that there is no one like him, and maybe there never will be.

And shame on my love, if I ever try to look for him in someone else. Was your choice really so common that I would find it in everyone?

Love is when, even with the whole world around you, you still ache for that one person alone. That is the kind of feeling of love I experienced after meeting ‘him’—after that, I stopped searching, but I could never stop asking for him in my prayers. After that, I never felt that moving on was the right thing, because I had loved truly. I never wanted him to become mine; after him, I only started looking for flaws within myself. I never looked at any other man because it feels as if I would be cheating on him. Yes, I know he is no longer with me, but my promises of loyalty, our conversations, everything still lives in my memory even today.

Some connections do not end with closure; they end by settling deep within us, shaping who we become. Not moving on does not mean being stuck in the past — it means carrying the past with dignity. It is waking up each day knowing that the person is no longer part of your present, yet refusing to turn your feelings into something casual or forgettable. In a world that constantly pushes us to heal fast and love again quickly, choosing not to move on becomes an act of resistance, a way of saying that some bonds are too rare to be repeated. Not moving on is also about loyalty — to memories, to promises once spoken, and to the version of yourself that loved sincerely. It is choosing solitude over shallow Connections, depth over distraction.

You stop searching for the same feeling in new faces because you know love is not transferable; it cannot be copied or recreated in someone else. There is pain in this choice, yes, but there is also peace. The peace of knowing you did not betray your emotions, that you did not rush your healing just to fit into society’s idea of “normal”. The art of not moving on teaches patience, emotional honesty, and the courage to live with longing without letting it destroy you. It is to accept that some bonds are meant to stay incomplete yet meaningful. Living with unfinished love is not about clinging to the past; it is about honoring a connection that changed you, even if it never stayed.

Living with unfinished love means learning how to breathe in the space where answers never arrived and endings were never clearly written. Unfinished love does not fade with time; it simply changes its place within you, moving from longing to memory, from ache to acceptance. You learn to smile in public while privately holding conversations that will never be spoken aloud. There are moments when the past returns gently — through a familiar laugh, a place once shared, a song you listened together and a thought that appears uninvited — and instead of resisting it, you let it pass through you. Living with unfinished love is about allowing it to shape your understanding of love while still continuing to live, grow and exist with quite strength.

“Staying loyal to the one who is gone is not about refusing life or denying the present; it is about honoring a love that shaped your soul. It is choosing to carry someone quietly within you, even when the world expects you to replace them, forget them or outgrow them. Loyalty, in the sense, is not loud or dramatic — it lives in small, invisible acts: in the way you still think of them before sleeping, in the way certain songs pause your breath. They are gone, yet they remain in your choices, your silences, your becoming.”

Section 08

Maybe in another lifetime

Maybe in another lifetime, you will be mine — this sentence is not a hope, but the final pause of an unfinished story that I repeat quietly within myself everyday. It is the acceptance of a love that could not be completed, because of time, circumstances and fear, yet whose truth still lives in every breath I take. In this lifetime, we met, conversations flowed like rivers, silences spoke just as deeply, eyes made promises, and hearts trusted without conditions. But fate, perhaps, had written a different script for us. That is why we were close, yet never truly together. Sometimes it feels as though the deepest form of love is the one that holds no ownership and no expectations — only acceptance. Acceptance that you should be happy, even if that happiness does not include me. Acceptance that your smile should not depend on my presence. Acceptance that I could not hold on to you, yet I learned how to love you even after losing you.

In this life, what we gained were moments — some bright, some blurred — and what we lost was a future. Still, the light of those moments continues to guide me through my darker days.
